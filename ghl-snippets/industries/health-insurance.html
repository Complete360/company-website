Who you serve, and why it's hard

The health insurance agent reality

You help three very different kinds of clients, each searching in different places at different times. Reaching all of them takes more than a busy open enrollment.

Families

What they need

Someone to explain their options in plain English and help them pick coverage that fits the household.

Why they're hard to reach

They shop once a year or after a big life change, and they're overwhelmed by ads from call centers.

Self-employed and gig workers

What they need

Guidance on individual coverage when there's no employer plan and income changes from year to year.

Why they're hard to reach

They're busy running their own work and often put coverage off until a deadline is close.

Small business owners

What they need

Help offering coverage to their team, and an agent who'll still be there at renewal time.

Why they're hard to reach

They pick advisors by reputation and referral, so a thin online presence costs you the meeting.

And for you:

Everything happens at once in the fall

Open enrollment fills every hour, then the phones go quiet. Calls you can't answer in the rush go to someone else.

Shared leads and compliance pressure

Bought leads are often sold to several agents, and every ad you run has to meet insurance marketing rules.

Health insurance agent marketing questions

How do health insurance agents get more clients?

Reach people when they need coverage help, look like the trusted local agent when they check you out, and stay in touch every year. For most agents that starts with a Google Business Profile with steady new reviews and Meta Ads with permission-based lead forms. An AI Voice Agent for open enrollment calls and a CRM for renewals come next.

Do Facebook ads work for health insurance agents?

They're a strong way to reach families, self-employed workers and small business owners in your area, especially around life changes and before open enrollment. We use lead forms that ask for permission to contact them, so the leads come to you directly instead of being shared with other agents.

How do you keep insurance ads compliant?

We write ads and pages without plan, price or subsidy promises, and every ad, landing page and form is sent to you for approval before it runs, so you can check it against your carrier and agency requirements. As the licensed agent, you have final say on compliance.

How can I get clients outside of open enrollment?

Focus on the moments that bring people to you all year: life changes that can open a special enrollment period, small businesses setting up or renewing coverage for their teams, and referrals from happy clients. Meta Ads and a strong Google Business Profile keep you visible for all three.

Can an AI Voice Agent answer calls during open enrollment?

Yes, for scheduling. It answers inbound calls, takes the caller's details and puts an appointment on your calendar. It doesn't discuss, compare or enroll anyone in plans; that conversation is yours. AI usage is unlimited with no per-minute AI fees. Your phone number and calling minutes are billed separately.

Why does a Google Business Profile matter for an insurance agent?

When someone hears your name from a friend or sees your ad, they look you up. A Google Business Profile with your office, hours and reviews shows them a real local agent, which matters to people who are tired of calls from out-of-state call centers.

Can you guarantee a number of leads or new clients?

No. No one can promise lead counts or enrollments. For Google Business Profile Management and advertising campaigns, including Meta Ads, if you're not satisfied at the end of the service period, we extend the campaign at no charge. That applies when onboarding was completed and approvals came in on time, you raise the concern at the end of the initial period, and your account is in good standing.

How much does health insurance agent marketing cost?

Each service's price is on its own page, including Google Business Profile Management, the AI Voice Agent and Complete360 CRM. Meta Ads are planned with you on a strategy call, and ad spend is billed directly by Meta. To cancel a recurring service, give at least 10 days' written notice before the next billing date. Payments are non-refundable from the time of purchase.
